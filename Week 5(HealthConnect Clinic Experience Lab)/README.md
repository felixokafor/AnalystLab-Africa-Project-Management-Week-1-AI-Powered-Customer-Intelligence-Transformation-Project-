Overview

Week 5 focused on moving the HealthConnect Clinic Experience Lab from the Week 4 planning foundation into an execution and progress-management stage.

As part of the Project Management track, the focus was on converting the Week 4 project structure into actionable tasks, establishing execution controls, monitoring risks and dependencies, documenting project issues and decisions, and preparing a coordination framework for the different workstreams.

Because this is an individual online internship assignment and direct cross-track status updates were not available, technical workstream completion is not claimed without supporting evidence.

The main objectives for Week 5 were to:

- Review the Week 4 project foundation and WBS.

Key Project Management Activities

1. Task-Level Execution Control

The Week 4 WBS was converted into actionable tasks that can be monitored by:

- Task ID
- Workstream
- Responsible Workstream
- Priority
- Dependencies
- Planned Timing
- Status
- Progress / Evidence

This created a clearer connection between the project's high-level structure and day-to-day execution.

2. Progress Monitoring

Evidence-based status categories were introduced to prevent unsupported project-status claims.

The main categories include:

- Completed — supported by project documentation or evidence.
- Planned — identified as required but not evidenced as completed.
- Awaiting Workstream Update — technical activity for which direct progress evidence is unavailable.
- At Risk — a documented condition creates a credible threat to delivery.
- Blocked — a confirmed blocker prevents progress.

3. Risk and Dependency Management

Week 4 risks and dependencies were reviewed and carried forward where they remained relevant.

Additional execution-stage governance considerations were identified, including:

- Limited visibility of technical workstream status.
- Unclear ownership where direct assignment evidence is unavailable.
- Inconsistency across separate project artefacts.
- Treating assumptions as confirmed project facts.
- Potential delays caused by unresolved workstream dependencies.

4. Issues and Decision Management

An Issues Log and Decision Log were introduced to strengthen project governance.

Key decisions included:

- Using Responsible Workstream instead of Assigned To where direct assignment evidence was unavailable.
- Using evidence-based status categories.
- Maintaining one central PM control workbook.
- Using relative project weeks rather than inventing calendar dates.

5. Track Coordination

No direct cross-track collaboration is claimed for Week 5.

Instead, expected coordination points were documented between Project Management and the technical workstreams.

Examples include:

- Project Management ↔ Data Analytics: Dataset/Data Dictionary inputs and analysis findings/KPIs.
- Project Management ↔ Data Science: Data readiness, modelling progress and results.
- Project Management ↔ ML Engineering: Requirements, implementation status and technical issues.
- Project Management ↔ Generative AI: Clinic Knowledge Base, prototype/testing evidence, safety and escalation requirements.
- Cross-workstream integration: Milestone outputs and dependent-workstream readiness.

The purpose is to provide a structured mechanism for monitoring handoffs, dependencies, blockers, and project alignment.

---

Key Insights

Week 5 demonstrated that effective project management requires more than maintaining a timeline.

The transition into execution requires:

- Clear task ownership or responsibility.
- Prioritisation.
- Evidence-based progress tracking.
- Active risk and dependency monitoring.
- Issue and decision management.
- Controlled communication and coordination.
- Clear distinction between confirmed information and assumptions.

«“Do not report progress that cannot be supported by evidence.”»

This was particularly important because technical workstream status was not directly available in the individual online internship setting.

---

Challenges

The main challenge was the absence of direct cross-track status updates.

This meant that technical progress could not be independently verified. Rather than creating unsupported completion percentages or claiming interactions that did not occur, the project control package uses appropriate status labels such as Planned and Awaiting Workstream Update.

Another challenge was maintaining consistency across multiple Project Management artefacts. A central PM control workbook was therefore used as the primary execution-control package.
